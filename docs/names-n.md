# Names that start with N

Written 2026-09-29. A second pass on `names.md`, limited to names starting with N so the project
and its language pair up: the N in N-Con stands for nested, and can quietly stand for the project
too.

## What I scored on

- **Lineage.** Spoon, soup, napkin: humble things at a table or in a kitchen, a little playful.
- **Fit with the design.** Nested concepts, a graph woven from small pieces, a tiny core, thinking
  it out honestly instead of guessing.
- **With N-Con.** Does "N-Con" read as "<name> concepts" or at least sit comfortably beside it?
- **Sound and memory.** One or two syllables, no spelling doubt when heard aloud.
- **As a tool.** `name "what's on today"`, a package name, `~/.name/`, `NAME.md`. Short and
  lowercase-friendly wins; a CLI people already have installed counts against it.
- **Conflicts.** The top 15 were checked on the npm registry (with weekly downloads), GitHub repo
  search by stars, Homebrew, and known AI products and CLIs. A dead npm placeholder barely matters
  (`@keal/name` or `name-core` solves it). A prominent product in the same space matters a lot.

## The ranked list

1. **Nappe.** Old French for tablecloth, and the word napkin is built from (nappe plus -kin), so it
   is literally napkin's parent. In the kitchen, a sauce is "nappe" when it coats the back of a
   spoon, which ties it straight back to the first ancestor. It also names a folded sheet of rock
   in geology, layers laid over layers, a fair picture of nested concepts. Against: people will say
   "nap" and some will hear "nappy". `nappe`, `~/.nappe`, `NAPPE.md` all look clean. Conflicts:
   npm free, GitHub has nothing named nappe, no Homebrew formula, no AI product. Wide open.

2. **Noodle.** Both halves at once: a kitchen staple, and "use your noodle", the head that works
   things out. A bowl of noodles is a tangle of strands that all connect, which is the graph. Playful
   and instantly memorable; "N-Con, noodle concepts" makes people smile. Against: slightly silly,
   and very common as a word. Conflicts: npm `noodle` is an old tweening library (44/wk);
   `noodle-run/noodle` is a student productivity app (~12k stars) and `unslop-xyz/noodles` is an
   AI-codebase tool (~1.3k stars). No Homebrew clash. Taken but not in the same space; moderate.

3. **Noggin.** A noggin is the head, and also a small wooden cup or a measure of drink at the
   table. Thinking plus tableware in one word, with a friendly old-fashioned sound. Against: a
   touch cartoonish, and Noggin was a Nick Jr kids' channel and app. Conflicts: npm `noggin` is a
   logging placeholder (2/wk); `fedora-infra/noggin` is Fedora's account portal (~150 stars);
   no AI product, no Homebrew. Obscure.

4. **Napery.** The collective word for table linen, napkins and tablecloths, from the same nappe.
   Keeps "nap" so the lineage shows plainly, and is the grown-up napkin. Against: few people know
   it, and it can feel like napkin wearing a hat. Conflicts: npm free, GitHub effectively empty,
   no products. Wide open.

5. **Nosh.** Food, a snack, "a bit of nosh". Four letters, perfect command (`nosh "add milk"`),
   `NOSH.md` reads well. Design fit is weak: it is all table and no mind. Conflicts: npm `nosh` is a
   Node shell (98/wk), which is the wrong kind of overlap for a CLI; NOSH is also an open-source
   health records system (~80 stars) and a food-ordering brand name in several places. Moderate.

6. **Nubbin.** A small leftover lump, the nub of bread or cheese left on the board. Tiny, humble,
   and "the nub of it" means the essential point, a fair picture of a tiny core. Against: odd, and
   hard to take seriously. Conflicts: npm free; GitHub only single-star repos; no products. Open.

7. **Nutshell.** "In a nutshell": the whole thing in something very small, which is the runtime.
   The nut is kitchen, the shell holds it. Against: eight letters (alias `nut`), and it is a phrase
   more than a name. Conflicts: npm `nutshell` placeholder (2/wk); Nutshell is a well-known sales
   CRM; `OSCPU/NutShell` is a RISC-V core (~1.5k stars) and `ncase/nutshell` an explainer tool
   (~620 stars). Crowded but none are AI assistants.

8. **Nest.** The strongest design fit of all: N-Con is nested concepts, and a nest is things woven
   into things. Nesting bowls and bird's nest soup keep a foot in the kitchen. Against: the
   conflicts are severe. Conflicts: npm `nest` taken (~19k/wk, an HTTP client); NestJS
   (~77k stars) ships a `nest` CLI that most Node developers have installed; Homebrew `nest` is
   the NEST neural simulator; Google Nest owns the word in consumer tech. Prominent clash, use
   only as an internal term.

9. **Nog.** Eggnog, the old word for strong ale, and the root of noggin. Three letters, the
   shortest command here. Against: little design meaning, and seasonal. Conflicts: npm `nog` is a
   dead weblog package (53/wk); GitHub has `google/nogotofail` and `nogil` but nothing called nog
   itself of note. Obscure.

10. **Nibble.** A small bite, and also half a byte, which makes it a nice nerd crossover.
    "It works it out a nibble at a time" suits the slow and honest personality. Against: cute
    rather than sturdy. Conflicts: npm `nibble` is a CSS placeholder (13/wk); no prominent repos
    or AI products. Obscure.

11. **Nutmeg.** A small hard seed you grate a little of into almost anything. Warm sound, very
    kitchen. Against: no design meaning, and Nutmeg is a well-known UK investing app. Conflicts:
    npm `nutmeg` dead (0/wk); no AI product. Obscure on npm, prominent brand in finance.

12. **Nook.** The breakfast nook, a small corner of the kitchen where you sit and think. Cosy.
    Against: Barnes and Noble's Nook e-reader, and `nook-browser/Nook` (~2k stars). Conflicts: npm
    `nook` placeholder (5/wk). Moderate.

13. **Nori.** The sheet that wraps everything together in a roll, which is what a concept does
    for its parts. Short and pretty. Against: loose design fit. Conflicts: npm `nori` is live
    (4.1.0, updated 2026-08, 14/wk); `mit-pdos/noria` and `norish-recipes/norish` are nearby but
    not the same word. Taken and active.

14. **Naan.** Bread from the oven, soft sound. Against: reads as NaN to every programmer, which is
    either a great joke or a constant confusion, and `isNaan` jokes already exist. Conflicts: npm
    `naan` is a currying library (259/wk). Obscure but punny in the wrong way.

15. **Nougat.** Layered, nutty, sweet: layers are a fine picture of nesting. Against: the AI
    clash is direct. Conflicts: `facebookresearch/nougat` is Meta's document-understanding model
    (~10k stars), and Android Nougat. npm `nougat` placeholder (5/wk). Prominent clash in AI.

16. **Niblet.** A tiny piece of food. Softer than nibble. npm free; GitHub has a small MCP design
    tool called niblet. Cute but slight.

17. **Nuncheon.** An archaic word for a light midday snack, the ancestor of luncheon. Wonderfully
    obscure, npm free, GitHub empty. Against: nobody knows it or can spell it on hearing it.

18. **Noshery.** A place to nosh. npm free. Playful but long and restaurant-ish.

19. **Nib.** The small tip of a pen, or a cacao nib. Writing tool fits a thing that writes its own
    graph. npm `nib` is Stylus mixins with ~100k/wk, so it is very taken.

20. **Navarin.** A French lamb stew, a direct follow-on from soup. npm free. Against: few know it,
    and it sounds like a naval rank.

21. **Nage.** A court-bouillon, the broth things are poached in, a quiet nod to soup. npm `nage`
    is an object pool (7/wk). Nobody will know how to say it.

22. **Noisette.** Hazelnut, and brown butter in French cooking. Pretty, but three syllables and
    French spelling.

23. **Nosebag.** The feed bag, and slang for a meal. npm free. Funny, but not a name you would
    want to type all day.

24. **Nacho.** Snack food, and "nacho problem" jokes write themselves. npm `nacho` is a CSS
    placeholder. Too jokey for a thing that should be honest.

25. **Nectar.** Sweet and good, but sounds like an AI marketing name, the opposite of the brief.

26. **Neep.** Scots for turnip, as humble as food gets. Four letters, but sounds like a noise.

27. **Nibs.** Cacao nibs, small pieces. npm taken. Plural names are awkward as commands.

28. **Nestling.** A young bird still in the nest: nested, and young, learning. npm taken
    (placeholder); GitHub only tiny repos. Nice, but long and a little precious.

29. **Nexus.** A connection point, very graph-like. Overused in tech (Sonatype Nexus, Google
    Nexus); sounds like an AI brand.

30. **Noesis.** Greek for the act of understanding. Strong meaning, zero table. npm taken.

31. **Noema.** Husserl's word for the content of a thought, which is close to what a reading is.
    But GitHub already has several AI-memory projects called Noema, and npm `noema` is an
    OpenAI component generator. In the exact space to avoid.

32. **Nomen.** Latin for name, and every word is a concept with a name. npm taken. Dry.

33. **Neume.** A medieval notation mark: a small written sign that stands for a sung meaning.
    Lovely and obscure, nobody will know how to say it.

34. **Nuance.** What the scorer is really for, picking the reading that fits. But Nuance
    Communications is a big speech-AI company (Dragon, now Microsoft). Prominent clash.

35. **Nous.** Greek for mind or intellect. Nous Research is a prominent open AI lab (Hermes
    models); an assistant called nous would be taken for theirs.

36. **Notion.** Exactly what the graph holds, notions. Notion the app is enormous. No.

37. **Node.** Every concept is a node. Also every JavaScript runtime on earth. No.

38. **Net.** A woven graph and a kitchen sieve. Too generic to search for or own.

39. **Nudge.** How corrections teach it, a nudge at a time. Behavioural-economics and many apps
    already use it; no table lineage.

40. **Nth.** Terse and nerdy, "to the nth degree". Unsearchable and unpronounceable as a word.

41. **Nimble.** A trait, not a thing, and contradicts the honest-and-slow personality.

42. **Natto.** Fermented soybeans: humble, divisive, alive. Very funny to people who have tried it.

43. **Nduja.** A spreadable sausage. Delicious, unspellable.

44. **Neapolitan.** Three flavours side by side in one block. Far too long.

## Top 5 and why

1. **Nappe.** The only name here that is napkin's actual ancestor, it touches spoon through the
   kitchen meaning, and its layered geology meaning fits nested concepts. Short, clean as a command
   and a folder, and completely free.
2. **Noodle.** The best "both" name: kitchen food and the head that thinks, with a tangle of
   strands as the graph. The npm name is taken and there is a popular unrelated app, so it would
   ship scoped.
3. **Noggin.** Head and tableware in one old word, friendly and memorable. Only obscure conflicts.
4. **Napery.** The safest lineage name, wide open, but less fun to say than Nappe and it covers the
   same ground, so it is Nappe's backup.
5. **Nosh.** The best pure command and the most casual table word; weak on design meaning and the
   npm `nosh` Node shell is a mild CLI overlap.

Nest has the best design fit of any word on the list, but the NestJS `nest` CLI and Google Nest
make it unusable as the project name. It would make a good name for a concept or a command inside
the project.
